How it's drawn

Pixels, palette, outlines

The scene is a real three.js 3D world seen through an orthographic isometric camera. It is rendered into a tiny render target with no anti-aliasing, then scaled up with nearest-neighbour filtering.

A post pass reads the depth buffer: where a pixel is much farther than its neighbours it is darkened, giving the outlines. Every pixel is then snapped to the nearest of 64 fixed colours, measured in OKLab so “nearest” matches what the eye sees. Only the sky uses ordered dithering.

Water is a wave-equation height field in ~20 cm cells. Its normals shade the surface, a mirrored copy of the whole lido gives the reflection, and the same field bends the caustics on the tiled floor.

Dives are flown, not animated: take-off speed and angular momentum are solved so the somersaults land; tucking shrinks the moment of inertia, so the spin speeds up, and kicking out slows it to line up the entry.
